The Deliverator belongs to an elite order, a hallowed sub-category. He's got esprit up to here. Right now, he is preparing to deliver a pizza.

His car is a 1994 Pontiac, a pizza delivery vehicle. There are a lot of cars out there that are a lot better. The Deliverator does not care. He drives the car that people like.

The DELIVERATOR used to make software. But that field went south, and he had to retool. Now he delivers pizzas. But he is the best in the business. Nobody delivers a pizza faster.

The Deliverator stands tall, arms at his side. The pizza box in the back seat is emitting a small amount of heat. The car idles. The pizza has thirty minutes to get there.

Twenty-nine minutes and fifty-eight seconds, to be exact. The Deliverator knows this because a small LED timer on the pizza box shows the elapsed time since the order was phoned in.

He is going to make it. No problem.

The pizza was ordered by a customer at a huge, gated, walled housing development. The Deliverator has been making runs out to this place for years. He knows the route like the back of his hand.

Turn out of the parking lot. Head south on Main Street. Take a right on Jefferson. Cross the railroad tracks. Take the second left.

The development has its own name: White Columns. There are white columns in front of all the houses, and it's all very southern and beautiful.

Except that the road leading up to White Columns is under construction. They are widening it. This makes it a bumpy ride.

The Deliverator hates the road to White Columns. It's torn up. It's full of debris. There are steel plates over trenches, and they clang like castanets under his tires. But it doesn't matter, because he knows where all the bumps and holes are.

He dodges. He weaves. He executes moves that make him glad he has the Pontiac. Because a Pontiac can absorb a lot of punishment, and its suspension was designed by someone who loved driving.

The gate of White Columns opens automatically. The Deliverator is in the system. The gate recognizes the Pontiac, or more specifically, it recognizes the transmitter that is clipped to the sun visor.

He pulls into the driveway of a Colonial. Big columns. Two stories. He's delivered here before. Nice people. Big tippers.

He mounts the steps, pizza box in hand. The door opens before he can ring the bell. It's the teenage daughter.

"Pizza!" she says.

He hands it over. She gives him the money. Including a very generous tip.

"Thanks!" he says, and hustles back to the Pontiac.

Twenty-six minutes and thirty seconds. Not bad. Not bad at all.

The LED timer on his dashboard confirms it. He has successfully delivered another pizza within the sacred thirty-minute window. The system records his time automatically.

The Deliverator is the best. He knows it. His customers know it. Even the management knows it, though they would never admit it.

He is the Michael Jordan of pizza delivery. The Wayne Gretzky. The Hakeem Olajuwon. He doesn't just deliver pizzas. He DELIVERS pizzas.

And now, cruising back through the darkened streets, windows down, wind in his hair, he feels that familiar surge of satisfaction. Another delivery complete. Another customer served. Another data point in his perfect record.

The night is young. The phone will ring again soon. And when it does, the Deliverator will be ready.

He is always ready.
